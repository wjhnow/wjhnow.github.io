So researchers just published this in Neurology and I keep thinking about it. They took 60 people, all 60+ with mild to moderate Alzheimer's. Plugged them into sleep machines for one night. Gross. Then the next morning took spinal fluid. Yeah, spinal tap.

What were they looking for? This chemical called orexin. It’s basically what tells your brain "stay awake" or "go sleep". And they also measured deep sleep waves - the ones where you actually remember stuff and 3 years later they checked who got worse.

Here’s where it gets interesting!!

People with high orexin? Their Alzheimer's progressed faster. Not great. BUT - and this is the part I like - the people who still had strong deep sleep waves seemed protected. Like even with high orexin, if your sleep spindles were dense, the decline was slower. Stronger spindles = more protection. That’s basically it.

Weird detail: women had way more orexin in their fluid than men did. No idea why.

Now the reality check. 60 people is nothing. One night of sleep isn’t “data”. And they didn’t even have a healthy group to compare to. So we can’t say “this cures anything”. The reason this isn't just another forgotten study is that orexin drugs already exist — they're not starting from scratch.. Used for narcolepsy. So if deep sleep really does buffer the brain... maybe that’s a path?

Tbh we need way bigger studies before getting excited.
