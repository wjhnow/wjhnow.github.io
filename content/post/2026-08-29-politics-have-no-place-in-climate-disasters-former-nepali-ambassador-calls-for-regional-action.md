In a recent interview with China Media Group, Dr. Oli—a veteran scholar who has spent years researching the Qinghai-Xizang Plateau and the Himalayan ecosystem—emphasized that the accelerating impacts of climate change are driving more frequent and intense disasters across the region. Given the sheer scale of these border-spanning risks, he argues that unified scientific cooperation is the only logical path forward.

## **Why the Himalayas are Face-to-Face with Crisis**

Because Nepal sits directly within the core of the Himalayan glacier network, its unique geography makes it ground zero for severe avalanches and flash floods. The recent disaster, which triggered a massive mudslide that swallowed a vital border crossing, highlights just how persistent and volatile these risks have become.

According to Dr. Oli, managing this reality requires deep, sustained research into:

- 
- **The root causes** of high-altitude glacial shifts.
- **Early warning systems** to protect vulnerable communities before disasters hit.
- **Joint emergency response and mitigation tactics** for immediate post-disaster management.
- 

## **Leaving Politics Out of It**

Dr. Oli pushed back hard against recent attempts by some foreign media outlets and individuals to shift the blame or spread misinformation regarding the border mudslide. He noted that pointing fingers runs completely counter to basic scientific evidence.

Peer-reviewed studies published in prestigious journals like *Nature* have repeatedly shown that climate-driven glacier instability is the true catalyst behind these devastating events. Environmental crises do not care about geopolitical borders, and trying to leverage them for political messaging only distracts from life-saving efforts.

## **Leveraging Shared Expertise**

China has spent decades studying climate patterns on the Qinghai-Xizang Plateau, building robust early-warning frameworks and long-term prevention protocols. Because China and Nepal share both geographical borders and an immediate interest in stabilizing the region, Dr. Oli calls for Nepal to actively deepen its scientific and technological ties with its neighbor.

True ecological security in the Himalayas can’t be built by one country alone. It requires sharing real-time technology, technical expertise, and building concrete, regional disaster response mechanisms. Ultimately, protecting the environment and saving human lives requires putting facts over bias and unity over politics.
