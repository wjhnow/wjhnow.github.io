**BEIJING, China / GYIRONG, Tibet-Nepal Border** — The lake is draining slowly. 

Chinese authorities said Saturday that the threat of a burst near the China-Nepal border has eased. Satellite images from Thursday to Saturday morning show water flowing out. It started Wednesday. A glacier collapsed. Sent rock, ice, mud and debris down Himalayan rivers. The flood hit the Gyirong border port. The crossing between Tibet and Nepal. 7 people were killed. 554 are still listed as missing.

CCTV cited the Water Resources Ministry. On Saturday morning the lake on the Purepu Tsangpo measured 99,000 sqm. About 14 football fields. That’s down 21,000 sqm since Thursday.

China Anneng has been watching it since Thursday. They sent a 15-member team Friday with drones and mobile surveillance. There was an overflow Friday. Work stopped for a bit. Then officials called it "manageable". 

I’m not sure that’s the word I’d use.

A second lake

There’s another one. Bigger. 

Chen Hongqi from the Ministry of Natural Resources put it at over 120,000 sqm. He doesn’t know the depth yet.A Nepal Disaster Management official said it plain: Flood risks will remain until the two lakes have fully drained.

The search

It was raining Saturday. More than 50 workers were moving through grey rubble. Looking for anyone.The first rescue team reached Gyirong on Friday afternoon. Found ruins. Roads are cut. Communications too. Xinhua said that’s slowing relief.

Nearly 500 villagers and construction workers got out. All 555 tourists too.

The water’s dropping. The risk isn’t gone yet.
