**BEIJING, China / GYIRONG, Tibet-Nepal Border** — The threat of a glacial lake burst near the China-Nepal border has decreased, Chinese authorities said on Saturday (Aug 29), as satellite imagery shows the reservoir is slowly draining.

The lake formed after a glacier collapse on Wednesday sent a torrent of rock, ice, mud and debris through Himalayan river systems. The flood swept away the Gyirong border port complex linking China's Tibet to Nepal, killing 7 and leaving 554 missing.

According to state broadcaster CCTV citing the Water Resources Ministry, satellite images from Thursday to Saturday morning show water has been flowing out of the lake.

The lake on the Purepu Tsangpo river measured 99,000 sqm on Saturday morning, roughly 14 football fields. That’s down 21,000 sqm since Thursday.

State-owned rescue firm China Anneng has been monitoring the lake since Thursday and sent a 15-member team on Friday with drones and mobile surveillance. An overflow on Friday briefly halted rescue work before risks were deemed manageable.

Authorities also flagged a second, larger lake at the bottom of the collapse. Chen Hongqi of the Ministry of Natural Resources estimates it at over 120,000 sqm, with depth still unknown.

"Flood risks will remain until the two lakes have fully drained," an official with Nepal's Disaster Management Authority said.

In rainy conditions Saturday, more than 50 workers searched grey rubble for survivors. The first rescue team reached the Gyirong border crossing on Friday afternoon and found only ruins.

Roads and communications remain cut off, complicating relief efforts, Xinhua reported. Nearly 500 villagers and construction workers plus all 555 tourists have been evacuated from the area.
