ABA - The town sits right on the edge. 

Not technically "Tibet" on the map. But it feels like it.

Last year the BBC went there. Just outside what China calls the Tibetan Autonomous Region. To try to understand what life is like.

They spoke to monks at Kirti monastery. Aba, or Ngaba in Tibetan. It used to be the center of resistance to Beijing.

“We Tibetans are denied basic human rights,” one monk told them.

The conversations were brief. On purpose. 

They said they live under constant surveillance. Claimed the Chinese government oppresses and persecutes Tibetans.

You don’t get long interviews like that. Not there.

Beijing’s response is different. They deny violating rights. Point to the money instead. Heavy investments, tourism, roads linking it to the rest of the country.

I don’t know which version people inside China are hearing. Probably not this one.

The BBC noted it plainly: people in China will not know very much about what is happening in Tibet right now.

Information doesn’t travel the same way across that line. 

The monks kept it short. Then it was over.
